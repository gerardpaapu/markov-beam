# Greed, fairness and why 4 is exactly right

I've been trying to learn candle, a rust library like torch for
Machine Learning which is a new topic me.

The problem with trying to learn about new things, is you don't know
anything about them. Which sounds like it should make it easier but 
it just doesn't. I've been programming for almost a kajillion years
and still almost every domain of programming is a foreign country.

Like when you're in a foreign country, learning a new subject hits two big snags:

## Number 1: You don't speak the language

Without the basic vocab, it's hard to even get off the ground. You ask someone "What's a flurble, I was told I would need one." and they reply "Oh, don't worry about it. A flurble is just a jombo with the pleeg reversed". So you smile and nod, and back away slowly.

And you still don't know what a flurble is.

## Number 2: Your intutions are wrong

You finally get a jombo, locate what you think is the pleeg and start twisting it. A shocked flurble expert stops you and grabs the jombo. "What are you doing?" they ask. Stuttering you reply, "I was ummm reversing the pleeg... I need a flurble".

Taken aback, they look at you like you're an idiot and gently press the pleeg in with their thumb. Scoffing "Why would anyone twist a pleeg".

The things that seem natural to you, the instincts that make you an expert in one field, because you've trained them deep into your brain until you do them wihtout thinking, can suddenly become a liability when you're in a new domain.

## So ... what is a flurble?

To me, all the words involved in Machine Learning are "flurble", I've probably been using the word token in the context of parsing strings into structures since before rocks were invented, but that's not entirely what is meant by "token" when we're talking about LLMs.

Worse are the completely unfamiliar words, like logit. You look up logit and the answer is it's a number that means ... something, okay.

So what do you do with a logit? You turn it into a different number.
